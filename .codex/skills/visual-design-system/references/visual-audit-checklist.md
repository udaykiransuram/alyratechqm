# Visual Audit Checklist

Use this checklist when the user asks for a broad redesign, says the UI feels basic, or wants a more premium website without specifying exact changes.

## 1. Color System

- Is there a clear difference between page background, card surface, and elevated surface?
- Is the primary action color distinct, accessible, and used sparingly?
- Do accent, success, warning, and danger feel related to the palette?
- Are there too many unrelated hues competing for attention?
- Do text, muted text, and borders have a clear hierarchy?

## 2. Button Quality

- Is the primary action instantly obvious?
- Are secondary, ghost, and destructive actions visually distinct?
- Are button sizes consistent across the product?
- Do hover, active, focus, disabled, and loading states all exist?
- Do buttons feel deliberate in padding, height, radius, and contrast?

## 3. Shape Language

- Do buttons, inputs, cards, menus, and modals share the same radius family?
- Does the interface feel intentionally sharp, soft, or balanced?
- Are outlines, borders, and shadows consistent?

## 4. Spacing And Rhythm

- Is there a repeatable section rhythm from top to bottom?
- Are headers, filters, cards, and tables aligned cleanly?
- Does the page breathe without wasting space?
- Are dense admin surfaces still easy to scan?

## 5. Typography

- Is heading hierarchy clear at a glance?
- Are labels, helper text, and body text easy to separate?
- Are line lengths controlled?
- Does the page feel readable before it feels decorative?

## 6. Data-Heavy Screens

- Are tables easy to scan row by row and column by column?
- Are filters, bulk actions, and row actions obvious?
- Is the page’s main task visible above the fold?
- Are empty states and no-result states clear and useful?

## 7. Forms

- Are labels, helper text, validation, and required fields obvious?
- Do inputs, selects, and textareas feel like one system?
- Are primary and secondary actions clear?
- Are success, warning, and error messages easy to notice without shouting?

## 8. States

Check these explicitly:

- loading
- empty
- error
- success
- disabled
- selected
- archived or locked
- destructive confirmation

## 9. Mobile

- Are tap targets large enough?
- Do forms stack cleanly?
- Do tables degrade gracefully?
- Are sticky actions, menus, and bottom actions usable on smaller screens?
- Does the page feel designed for mobile, not merely compressed?

## 10. Prompt Modifier Map

Map vague style words into concrete design choices:

- `premium`: calmer palette, stronger hierarchy, cleaner spacing, less noise
- `top-tier`: consistent tokens, stronger controls, better states, polished rhythm
- `trustworthy`: deeper neutrals, restrained accent, clearer contrast
- `modern`: crisp type, simpler shapes, tighter visual logic
- `bold`: stronger contrast and typography, not necessarily louder colors
- `student-friendly`: slightly brighter accent, clearer progress and feedback, still credible

Use this as a rubric, not a rigid script.
